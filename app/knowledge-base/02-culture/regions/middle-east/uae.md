# United Arab Emirates

## FILE ROLE & METHOD

This file is the UAE's country file: one national staging brief with five
labeled internal zones. One TCCC hero beverage per scene, chosen by the
brief (see HERO PRODUCT SLOT), staged against real UAE dishes, vessels and
settings.

**Who the scene is about.** The UAE is two food cultures at once. About
nine in ten residents are expatriates (Indians the largest group, then
Pakistanis, Bangladeshis, Filipinos, Egyptians and other Arabs, and
Western Europeans); about one in ten is Emirati. Everyday eating is
dominated by expat food (shawarma, biryani, cafeteria parathas, burgers,
pizza, delivery), while Emirati dishes (machboos, harees, thareed,
luqaimat) carry the national, family and Ramadan occasions. Every scene
should know which of the two it is staging. [MEDIUM · S1]

**Scope.** Lunch, dinner, snacks, street food and delivery are in scope.
Breakfast is out of scope by default; chebab, regag and manakish appear
only as all-day café or snack items. Celebrations and game night are in
scope. No beverage other than the hero TCCC product is staged; gahwa,
karak and the rest are documented in ICONIC BEVERAGES.

### Structural decision: one file, five zones (recommendation — reviewer has final say)

One file, `uae.md`, with zones rather than a regional split (§1.1). The
food grammar (Emirati rice-and-meat dishes over a dense expat layer) is
shared across all seven emirates; what changes by place is the setting
(tower city vs villa suburb vs oasis vs coast) and how conservative the
staging should be (Sharjah is legally stricter than Dubai). That is a
zone callout, not a separate file. [EDITORIAL]

### Zones (one scheme, used throughout)

- **Dubai (default):** towers (Marina, JLT, JVC, Downtown, Business Bay),
  older low-rise districts (Karama, Satwa, Deira, Bur Dubai) and villa
  suburbs (Mirdif, Al Barsha, Jumeirah). The biggest delivery market and
  the most mixed population; beach, boardwalk and food-truck registers.
- **Abu Dhabi city:** the capital; more Emirati households in view, wide
  boulevards, corniche, villas with majlis rooms. Heritage dishes (chebab,
  regag, thareed) are strongly associated with the capital in Michelin and
  heritage sources. [MEDIUM · S2]
- **Al Ain & the interior:** the oasis city on the Omani border. Date
  palms, falaj channels, mud-brick and stone heritage, farms; the most
  traditional register; winter desert camping. [MEDIUM · S3]
- **Sharjah & the Northern Emirates (Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al
  Khaimah):** Sharjah is a dry emirate (alcohol banned since 1985) with a
  decency code written into law; push clothing and mixed-gender choices to
  the safe end here. Many Dubai workers live in Sharjah and Ajman
  apartments. [MEDIUM · S4]
- **East Coast (Fujairah, Khor Fakkan, Dibba):** the Gulf of Oman side;
  mountains behind the coast, fish markets, grilled fish and fish
  machboos. [MEDIUM · S5]

### Default when no zone is named

Dubai, evening, a young mixed-nationality group in a tower apartment for
delivery food; or, for an Emirati dish, an Emirati villa dining room with
machboos in the centre. Hero SKU per brief.

## HARD STAGING RULES — UAE (never stage; read before any scene)

- **Halal by default, no pork, no alcohol.** Pepperoni and sausage are beef
  or chicken; no bacon, no ham. No wine glass, beer bottle, bar, bottle
  shelf or tap in any scene, even in Dubai venues that are licensed. The
  UAE advertising guide bans alcohol advertising "directly or obliquely".
  Coca-Cola never appears as a mixer. Dubai's licensed "brunch" is
  drinking-led and is never staged. [HIGH · S7]
- **No shisha, no tobacco.** Shisha cafés are a real part of evening life,
  but the advertising rules ban tobacco in all forms: no hookah, coals or
  smoke in frame. [HIGH · S7]
- **Public morality and UAE values.** Modest clothing (see TALENT &
  CLOTHING), no intimate contact or couple poses, no legible slogans on
  clothes. [MEDIUM · S7, S8]
- **No flags in hero position, no leaders' portraits.** National Day car
  decoration and portraits are real but never staged; never a full flag;
  flag-colour bunting or ribbon soft in the background at most.
- **Religious imagery stays out.** No mosque interiors, prayer in progress,
  Quran or calligraphy in sharp focus; a prayer rug folded on a shelf at
  most. Diwali and Christmas scenes stop at the food and lights (no puja
  altar, no nativity).
- **Project rules hold:** nothing legible (menus, signs, packaging,
  screens), no brand marks except the brief's hero SKU, no delivery-app or
  restaurant branding unless the brief asks, no landmark reproduced as a
  recognisable building (say "a lit tower skyline", never a named tower),
  adults 21+, nobody recognisable, no identifiable children, at most two or
  three background faces, none sharp, nobody drinks from the hero product.

#### Venue: Tower apartment living room, Dubai Marina / JLT / JVC (home, indoor, night) · app: Gen Z apartment
- Prompt-ready line: "A compact modern Dubai apartment at night: the
  meal sharp on a low light-oak coffee table, behind it softly blurred a
  grey fabric sofa with plain cushions, a floor lamp's warm glow, a potted
  snake plant and floor-to-ceiling glass full of blurred high-rise lights."
- Use for: home indoor; dinner and late night; 1, 2 or friends; delivery
  pizza, burgers, shawarma, game and movie nights. Young professionals rent
  furnished studios and one- or two-bedroom flats in towers; listings
  describe neutral white walls, light wood-look or marble-look floor tiles,
  open-plan kitchens with white high-gloss cabinets, and balconies.
  [MEDIUM · S9]
- Soft background (the core): floor-to-ceiling glass with tower lights as
  warm and white bokeh; a grey or beige sofa with a couple of plain
  cushions and a throw; a large wall TV as a soft dark rectangle or a
  colour glow with nothing readable; a floor lamp; a fiddle-leaf fig or
  snake plant; white walls with one or two framed prints (blank or
  abstract); light porcelain floor tile with a flat-weave rug. Light: warm
  lamp plus city glow at night; hard white daylight by day. Palette: warm
  grey, beige, light oak, city-night blue. Signature shapes: the window
  wall of lit towers, the low sofa line, the TV rectangle, the lamp.
  [MEDIUM for the apartment shell; EDITORIAL for the styling]
- Shell: concrete tower, 2.6–2.8 m ceilings, a white ceiling with
  recessed downlights and a linear AC grille, sliding glass to a balcony.
- The table as set here: a low coffee table, oak or white marble-look; the
  delivery boxes open on it (plain kraft, no branding), paper napkins,
  small dip cups, a TV remote or game controller, small plates if used.
- Variants: Sharjah and Ajman apartments are older and larger, with
  heavier curtains and darker furniture; JVC and Business Bay are newer
  and more styled. Q4 to spring: balcony door open or the scene on the
  balcony with a small bistro table (no barbecue on balconies).
- Hallucination traps: a US suburban basement; a New York brick loft; a
  majlis floor setup for this crowd; a beer fridge; a recognisable tower
  (draw a generic lit skyline); a readable game on the TV.
- Never stage: alcohol, shisha, legible screens, branded delivery bags or
  boxes, couple poses.
- Confidence: MEDIUM shell, EDITORIAL styling. [S9, S10]

#### Venue: Emirati villa dining room (home, indoor, midday or evening) · app: Dining room
- Prompt-ready line: "An Emirati villa dining room: the shared platter of
  machboos sharp on a long rectangular dark-wood table, behind it softly
  blurred cream walls, heavy gold-and-beige curtains over sheers, a carved
  wood sideboard with a brass dallah coffee pot and a dish of dates, and a
  crystal chandelier glow."
- Use for: home indoor; Friday family lunch (the big meal), iftar, Eid,
  National Day, guests; 2 to a large family (snapshot rule). Many Emirati
  families now eat at a table on chairs with individual plates; the
  traditional floor meal in the majlis continues for large gatherings
  (see the next profile). [MEDIUM · S11]
- Soft background (the core): smooth cream, beige or warm white walls,
  often with gypsum cornices and a recessed ceiling with warm cove light;
  a large crystal or brass chandelier over the table; tall windows with
  heavy curtains (gold, beige, champagne, sometimes deep red or teal) over
  white sheers; a carved or lacquered sideboard with a dallah coffee pot,
  small handleless finjan cups on a tray, a covered date dish and an
  incense burner (mabkhara, unlit or a thin wisp); a framed abstract or
  geometric panel (no readable calligraphy). Floor: large polished marble
  or porcelain tiles, a patterned rug beyond the table. Chairs: upholstered
  high-back chairs in velvet or brocade. Light: by day bright light
  through sheers; by night warm chandelier and cove light. Palette: cream,
  gold, walnut, brass. Signature shapes: the chandelier, the curtain
  stack, the dallah on the sideboard, the long table. Density: spacious,
  formal, tidy. [MEDIUM for majlis and furnishing cues (design guides);
  EDITORIAL for the dining-room layout · S12]
- Shell: a two-storey villa, high ceilings (3–3.5 m), marble floors; a
  doorway to the family room or the majlis behind.
- The table as set here: a long rectangular wooden table, a glass or
  plastic protective cover in everyday use, or a cloth for guests (never a
  white tablecloth look); the centrepiece rice platter (round steel or
  white porcelain, 45–60 cm) with meat on top; bowls of harees, thareed or
  saloona; a salad bowl; a stack of regag or khameer; dates in a small
  bowl; individual plates, spoons and forks; a tissue box; glasses of
  water or laban. Hero SKU per brief.
- Variants: Abu Dhabi and Al Ain villas are more traditional (heavier
  fabrics, more gold); newer Dubai villas lean modern (stone feature wall,
  neutral sofas, LED cove light, champagne metal). [MEDIUM · S12]
- Hallucination traps: a Moroccan riad with zellige and pierced lanterns;
  an Indian thali spread; a Western white-cloth dinner party; a palace
  banquet; a desert tent as the default.
- Never stage: alcohol, a round table, readable calligraphy or
  portraits, a full flag, a prayer in progress, recognisable faces in
  photos.
- Confidence: MEDIUM-LOW (design-guide sources, no photo review). [S11, S12]

#### Venue: Majlis floor meal (sufra) (home, indoor)
- Prompt-ready line: "An Emirati majlis floor meal: platters of rice and
  meat massed on a plastic sheet over a patterned carpet, floor cushions
  and bolsters along cream walls softly blurred behind, a dallah and dates
  at the edge."

  cameras sit at seated table height, so a floor meal can't be drawn yet.
- Use for: large family gatherings, guests, weddings, Ramadan, Al Ain and

  finjan cups; an incense burner; tall curtained windows.
- The floor as set: a plastic or cloth sufra sheet; one or two large rice
  platters in the centre with meat on top, bowls of harees and salad
  around, dates, bread; people eat from the shared platter with the right
  hand or a spoon.
- Hallucination traps: a Bedouin tent with camels; a Japanese low table;

- Confidence: MEDIUM.

#### Venue: Cafeteria (كافتيريا) (street, day and night) · app: Counter service, Street market

### GENERAL NORMS

- **Rice and meat are the Emirati grammar.** Machboos (spiced rice with
  meat or fish on top, dried lime) is the everyday family centrepiece;
  harees (wheat and meat pounded smooth) and thareed (stew over regag
  bread) are Ramadan and festive essentials; luqaimat is the sweet.
  [HIGH · S26]
- **Bread:** regag (paper-thin crisp sheet, griddle-cooked), khameer
  (soft, slightly sweet, often date or fennel flavoured), Arabic pita and
  saj from the expat layer, paratha and parotta from the South Asian layer.
  [MEDIUM · S27]
- **Spice:** bzar (Emirati blend: cumin, coriander, cinnamon, cardamom,
  black pepper, dried chilli, turmeric), loomi (whole black dried lime),
  saffron, rose water. [MEDIUM · S26]
- **Dates everywhere:** a small bowl of dates sits on most Emirati tables
  and every iftar. [HIGH · S24]
- **Serving:** shared central platters, each diner with a plate at a
  table; spoon and fork (rice is also eaten with the right hand in
  traditional settings); knives rare. [MEDIUM · S11]
- **Delivery is central.** More than 47 million burgers were ordered on
  Talabat UAE in 2025; a mini cheese pizza was the single most-ordered
  item; evening delivery peaks 8–11 pm. Delivered food is a real home
  register here, not a shortcut. [MEDIUM · S20]
- **Weekend:** Saturday–Sunday since 2022, with Friday afternoon as the
  start. The Friday family lunch at relatives' homes remains the Emirati
  weekly peak; brunch shifted to Saturday. [MEDIUM · S28]

## CELEBRATIONS & LARGE GATHERINGS

**How large gatherings work here.** Emirati gatherings are big (a family
home can host 30 or more), with men and women often in separate rooms or
halls; meals are shared platters on a long table or a floor sufra. Expat
celebrations are home parties in apartments or community events in
restaurants. Headcounts below are editorial. Staging (§5.7): 1 setting =
one plate with shared platters cropped beyond; 2 = two facing plates with
centre platters; group = 3–4 plates at the table end running out of
frame. Never: alcohol, a full flag, leaders' portraits, prayer, mosque
interiors, identifiable children.

#### Celebration: Iftar (Ramadan evening)
- Type: religious, nightly (family and community).
- When: sunset during Ramadan; the intake's evening slot, dark outside.
- Gathering: an Emirati family table of 8–20; also hotel Ramadan tents and
  majlis buffets, and Karama's Ramadan street food festival (South Asian
  stalls, dishes from about AED 5). [MEDIUM · S25]
- The spread: dates and water to break the fast, laban, Vimto or jallab

  traditionally shared with neighbours. [HIGH · S25, S26]
- Snapshot staging: a stretch of the villa table at dusk with a date bowl
  per setting, the harees bowl and thareed bowl cropped, warm lamp light;
  window dark blue.
- Decor and cues: lanterns (fanous) unlit or softly lit, crescent motifs
  soft in the background, a dallah on the sideboard.

## DISH CATALOG

#### Dish: Machboos (مجبوس — national rice dish)
- Prompt-ready line: "A large round platter of machboos: a mound of long,
  separate rice grains tinted golden-orange, chicken or lamb pieces
  glossy and browned laid on top, scattered with crisp fried onion,
  golden raisins and toasted almonds, a whole black dried lime at the
  edge; the hero product beside it about half the platter's radius tall."
- Category: everyday family meal and festive centrepiece.
- Cuisine lineage: Gulf (cousin of kabsa and biryani); the dried lime
  (loomi) sets it apart. [HIGH · S26]
- Regional form variation: protein varies (chicken, lamb, goat, camel,
  fish, shrimp); fish machboos on the coasts. Prevalence-only.
- Preparation: rice cooked in the spiced meat broth (bzar, loomi,
  saffron, turmeric, tomato, sometimes rose water); meat cooked first,
  then laid on top. [MEDIUM · S26]
- Serving vessel: round steel or white porcelain platter, 45–60 cm.
- Utensils: spoon and fork at a table; right hand on the floor sufra.
- Visual/plating: rice mounded, meat on top, onions, raisins and nuts
  scattered; salad and a tomato-chilli salsa (dakkus) on the side.
- Common confusion: Saudi kabsa (near-identical; machboos uses loomi and
  bzar), Indian biryani (layered, often with whole spices and mint, not
  meat on top), Iraqi or Yemeni mandi (pit-smoked). [MEDIUM]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL]:
  - What dominates: rice ~65% of the visible surface, meat ~25%,
    garnish ~10%.
  - Components: 4–6 chicken pieces (each 8–12 cm) or lamb chunks on bone;
    fried onion strands; 15–25 raisins; 10–20 almond slivers; 1–2 loomi.
  - Arrangement: meat heaped in the centre, garnish scattered over.
  - Vessel fill: rice fills the platter to 3–5 cm from the rim, domed.
  - Served portion: a plate of rice with one piece of meat on top.
  - State cues: rice glossy with ghee, steam, meat juices.
  - Absent on purpose: mint leaves, boiled eggs, cashew-heavy biryani
    look, yoghurt raita on top.

#### Dish: Harees (هريس)
- Prompt-ready line: "A wide bowl of harees: a smooth, thick, beige
  porridge of pounded wheat and meat, its surface swirled and pooled with
  golden melted ghee, a light dusting of cinnamon; plain and rustic."
- Category: Ramadan, Eid, weddings; UNESCO-inscribed heritage practice.
  [HIGH · S30]
- Preparation: wheat soaked overnight, cooked about four hours with meat
  (lamb or chicken), bones removed, beaten until neither grain nor meat is
  visible; served with hot ghee poured over. [HIGH · S30]
- Serving vessel: a deep wide bowl or clay pot; shared.
- Visual: uniform beige, dense, smooth, slightly glossy; the ghee pool is
  the signature.
- Common confusion: Indian haleem (browner, spiced, garnished with fried
  onion, lemon and ginger); oatmeal. Negate fried onion and garnish.
- Composition [EDITORIAL]: porridge ~90%, ghee ~10%; fills the bowl to
  2 cm from the rim; spoon resting in it.

#### Dish: Thareed (ثريد)
- Prompt-ready line: "A deep bowl of thareed: thin crisp regag bread
  broken into pieces and soaked under a tomato-red stew of tender lamb,
  potato, marrow and carrot chunks, the bread edges still visible at the
  rim."
- Category: Ramadan centrepiece; family favourite; shared with neighbours.
  [HIGH · S25, S31]
- Preparation: saloona (spiced tomato stew with cumin, turmeric, bzar,
  loomi) poured over regag broken into the bowl just before serving.
  [MEDIUM · S31]
- Visual: red-orange gravy, chunky vegetables, meat on the bone, the
  soaked bread underneath.
- Common confusion: Iraqi tashreeb (similar idea, often with samoon and
  chickpeas), Levantine fatteh (yoghurt on top). Negate yoghurt.
- Composition [EDITORIAL]: stew and vegetables ~60%, bread ~25%, meat
  ~15%; bowl 25–30 cm.

#### Dish: Luqaimat (لقيمات)
- Prompt-ready line: "A small plate of luqaimat: a heap of craggy golden
  fried dough balls each about the size of a walnut, glazed with dark
  shiny date syrup and scattered with sesame seeds."
- Category: dessert and snack; iftar staple; festivals. [HIGH · S29]
- Preparation: yeasted batter with saffron and cardamom, fried in small
  spoonfuls until crisp outside and soft inside; date syrup (dibs) or
  honey, sesame. [HIGH · S29]
- Scale: each 3–4 cm; 12–20 on a plate. [EDITORIAL]
- Common confusion: Greek loukoumades (honey, cinnamon), Indian gulab
  jamun (dark, smooth, in syrup). Negate smooth surfaces and syrup baths.

#### Dish: Samak mashwi (سمك مشوي — grilled fish)
- Prompt-ready line: "A whole charcoal-grilled hamour fish on a long oval
  platter, skin charred and blistered with a red-gold spice paste, lemon
  wedges and sliced onion beside it, a mound of white rice and a small
  bowl of tomato-chilli salsa."
- Category: coastal everyday and family meal; East Coast and Abu Dhabi.
- Preparation: whole fish (hamour, kingfish, safi, seabream) rubbed with a
  paste of spices, dried lime and sometimes date paste, grilled over
  charcoal. [MEDIUM · S5]
- Scale: fish 30–45 cm on a 40–50 cm oval platter. [EDITORIAL]
- Common confusion: Iraqi masgouf (open-cut carp on stakes). Negate the
  butterflied staked fish.

#### Dish: Chebab with date syrup and cheese (all-day; breakfast scope)
- Prompt-ready line: "Three soft saffron-yellow Emirati pancakes stacked

- Category: heritage breakfast, also National Day and Ramadan; out of the
  default scope, used as an all-day café item. [MEDIUM · S27]
- Preparation: yeasted batter with saffron, cardamom, turmeric; griddled.
- Scale: 12–15 cm across. [EDITORIAL]

#### Dish: Regag with egg and cheese (snack)

#### Dish: Chicken shawarma wrap (شاورما)
