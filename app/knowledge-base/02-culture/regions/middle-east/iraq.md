# Iraq

## HARD STAGING RULES

These rules hold for every Iraq scene, whatever the brief. Every "never" also works as a negative prompt. (Baghdad pass, Fernando's rulings 13–15 Nov 2026.)

**City.** If the brief names no city, the scene is set in Baghdad: family houses in Mansour, Zayouna or Yarmouk, flats in Karrada, restaurants on the Karrada and Mansour streets, Zayouna and the Abu Nuwas riverside. When the brief names another city, drop every Baghdad profile; if that city isn't profiled yet (Basra, Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Duhok, Mosul, Najaf, Karbala), use the national content. Kurdistan scenes carry no Arabic signage. Never a generic "Middle East": no Gulf towers, Moroccan tiles or Levantine cafés.

**Legal, brand and political**
- No alcohol in any form: no bottles, glasses, bars or alcohol branding, and Coca-Cola is never a mixer.
- Pork: follow the meal the user submits. Avoid pork-forward dishes unless the brief names one or it is culturally acceptable in the setting (for example a Christian family celebration). Meat is halal by default; lamb, then chicken, beef, goat and fish. Pepperoni is beef or chicken. (Still to confirm with the client.)
- No brand marks except the hero SKU: no restaurant logos, and nothing copied from the matrix reference images. The prompt never says "Zinger" (a KFC trademark); it says spicy chicken fillet sandwich.
- No flags, political figures, party colours, militia or security insignia, portraits of leaders or slogans.
- No generated text: no Arabic, Kurdish or English on menus, signs, packaging, screens or clothing. Signs are soft colour blocks.

**Religious** (needs TCCC Iraq or legal sign-off)
- No shrines, domes of named mosques, religious figures, religious text or verse calligraphy in the hero zone, and no religious slogans on clothing. Framed calligraphy at home stays soft and unreadable.
- During Ramadan daylight hours, nobody eats or drinks in public scenes. Muharram and Ashura are mourning: no festive scenes on those dates.

**Custom and composition**
- Tea (istikan) stays out of the hero zone so the Coke is the only drink in focus. Nargileh stays in the far background or out.
- Shoes come off at the door in homes: no shoes on rugs or at a floor spread.
- Portions follow the pack. With a single-serve pack (250 mL glass bottle, 450 mL plastic bottle), one portion. Quzi, mandi, masgouf and mashawi are shared platters and always use the 1.75 L bottle, never the 250 mL glass.
- The app's "Sunday Lunch" means Friday lunch, the big family meal of the week.
- People: follow who the user's brief asks for; this file doesn't pick a cast. People are removed from scenes for now. No identifiable children.

## FILE ROLE & METHOD

This file is Iraq's country file: a single national staging brief with five
labeled internal zones. One TCCC hero beverage per scene (chosen per brief —
see HERO PRODUCT SLOT), staged against real Iraqi dishes, vessels and
settings, with the full drinks landscape documented as context even where a
drink is never itself staged.

**Scope.** Lunch (ghada), dinner (asha — the main family meal), snacks and
street food are in scope. Breakfast is out of scope by default (fatour:
qeimar + kahi, the full Iraqi breakfast); breakfast dishes appear only as
all-day bakery items (samoon, kleicha with tea), per the same logic as
`us-texas.md` breakfast tacos. No beverage other than the hero TCCC product
is staged; others are documented in ICONIC BEVERAGES.

### Structural decision: one file, five zones (recommendation — reviewer has final say)

One file, `iraq.md`, with an internal zone map rather than a regional split.
Rationale (§1.1): Iraq's variation resolves at the dish and ingredient level
(north pomegranate dolma; south fish; centre rice and sweets) within one
shared halal rice-and-stew grammar, not at the food + vocabulary +
architecture triple break that earned Scotland its own file. An
`iraq-kurdistan.md` spinout is the documented trigger if Kurdish-specific
usage ever exceeds ~30% of prompt volume — not an action item now.

### Zones (one scheme, used throughout)

- **Baghdad & Central (default):** Baghdad, Fallujah, Ramadi fringe. Timman
  anbar rice, masgouf on the Tigris, tashreeb, tepsi baytinijan. Urban
  apartment and riverside restaurant registers.
- **South & Basra / Marshes:** Basra, Nasiriyah, Amarah, the marshes
  (ahwar). Fish (masgouf, mutabbaq samak), dates and date syrup (dibis),
  kahi + qeimar breakfast culture. Palm-grove and corniche cues.
- **North / Mosul & Jazeera:** Mosul, Kirkuk, Tal Afar. Kubba Mosul (disc),
  the Mosul dolma pot (courgette, tomato, onion, pepper, aubergine and vine
  leaves steamed together), kubbat shorba in tomato-turnip-chard stew.
- **Kurdistan (Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Duhok):** pomegranate-forward dolma,
  hearty rice stuffings, mountain stone and timber cues; pork never;
  alcohol exclusion unchanged. Spinout candidate (see above).
- **West / Anbar steppe & Upper Euphrates:** tribal quzi (whole slow lamb +
  rice + nuts/raisins), outdoor mangal, tent-adjacent courtyard
  (genericized, no tribal insignia).

### Default when no zone is named

Baghdad (see HARD STAGING RULES): midday or evening family table with
timman anbar rice + stew, samoon on the side, hero SKU per brief.

  cuisine]
- **Sharbat:** date-based sharbat, tamarind and rose drinks at iftar and

### GENERAL NORMS

- **Rice is the starch grammar.** Long-grain timman anbar (aromatic,
  yellowish, Middle Euphrates) is the prestige rice; the "temmen" name's
  "Ten Men" brand folklore is vocabulary context. Multi-step cooking for
  fluffy grains, plus the hikakeh crisp bottom crust broken and shared, is
  the checkable technique. [HIGH] [S41]
- **Bread always on the table.** Samoon (diamond or pointed oval) plus the
  khubz / laffa / lavash / markook family; torn by hand. Tashreeb exists
  precisely to use bread: torn nan soaked in lamb or chicken-tomato broth
  in a big bowl. [HIGH] [S41]
- **Halal lamb default; no pork.** Chicken, beef, goat and fish follow.
  Stews over rice are the centre, not grilled-only. [HIGH]
- **Stew + rice + bread + pickle + yogurt pattern.** Fasolia yabsa (white
  beans + lamb or veal + tomato over rice), margat bamia (okra + lamb or
  beef + tomato), margat baytinijan / tepsi baytinijan (aubergine + meat +
  tomato, onion and garlic, potato slices on top, baked), qeema (mince +
  tomato + chickpea, Ashura-linked), jajik (strained yogurt + cucumber +
  garlic, dill or mint + olive oil), turshi pickles, amba mango pickle as a
  kebab and falafel dressing. [HIGH for the list; MEDIUM for exact
  everyday frequency]
- **North pomegranate, south fish, centre sweets.** The north adds
  pomegranate juice to dolma; the south centres on fish; the centre is
  known for rice and sweets (kleicha). [MEDIUM-HIGH] [S41]
- **Utensils:** spoon and fork (the spoon leads for rice and stew); knives
  rarely at the table; bread torn by hand; shared platters and individual
  bowls coexist. The communal flatbread under masgouf soaks up the juices
  — do not plate masgouf on rice by default. [MEDIUM — needs SME]

### TEXTURE LEXICON (use in prompts)

Fluffy separate rice + crisp hikakeh shards; char-blistered open-cut fish
with crisp skin and moist flake; silky okra-tomato stew without slime
strings; caramel-crisp potato on top of tepsi; crumbly date-filled kleicha
with an egg-wash sheen; torn samoon with an airy crumb and a crisp ridge.

### VISUAL & PLATING NORMS

- **Rice mounded, not moulded.** A fluffy timman pile with a saffron or
  turmeric tint where used, hikakeh shards at the edge; no ring-mould tower
  by default.
- **Stews glossy, tomato-based, oil-rimmed.** Bamia pods whole and intact;
  fasolia white beans whole; tepsi layered (fried aubergine + meat +
  tomato, onion and garlic + a potato top, baked). [MEDIUM-HIGH]
- **Masgouf open-cut, skin-on, stake-grilled.** Butterflied carp opened
  flat, scored, marinated (oil + salt + turmeric + tamarind), slow-roasted
  by the embers; garnished with lemon, onion and tomato + flatbread, not

#### Dish: Masgouf (national dish — Baghdad/Basra/Tigris)
- Prompt-ready line: "Half butterflied river carp about two can-heights
  long, amber turmeric glaze with crisp skin, laid over a round flatbread
  on a large steel tray with lemon, onion rings and tomato wedges. No
  rice, no fried batter, no alcohol."
- Baghdad serving: a shared platter for the table with the 1.75 L bottle, never a single portion or the 250 mL glass (HARD STAGING RULES).
- Prompt-ready line (briefing archive look): "A butterflied grilled
  carp, opened flat and golden-brown with dark char streaks, about one
  and a half bottle heights long, on a long dark-grey oval platter over
  rocket and lettuce with lemon wedges, pickled cucumber, black olives
  and red pepper strips. No batter, no rice."
- Category: Main; Friday and riverside restaurant signature.
- Cuisine lineage: Native Mesopotamian.
- Regional form variation: open fire on stakes in the centre and south; a
  clay-oven variant in the north.
- Authentic preparation (added 2026-10-09): a live carp (or shabout or
  bunni) is killed, scaled and split **along the back**, keeping the belly
  whole, so it opens flat like a book into a near-round fan. It's salted
  (purists stop there), or brushed with oil, salt, turmeric and tamarind
  or sumac. Two sharp iron stakes pin it upright, skin out, around a
  round fire pit of apricot or other fruit wood, a short distance from the
  flames, for roughly 45–60 minutes or more. At the end it's laid
  skin-down on the embers to crisp. Some cooks brush on a turmeric
  tomato-onion topping. Served on a tray over flatbread with lemon, raw
  onion, tomato, pickles and amba. [MEDIUM-HIGH · S49]
- Briefing note (TCCC AO Meals Phase 2, 2026-10-09): the briefing marked
  the archive masgouf **"reference is ok"**. That frame shows a
  **restaurant-plated** version: a half fish, golden-brown with dark char
  streaks, on a long dark-grey oval platter over a bed of rocket and
  lettuce with lemon wedges, pickled cucumbers and turnip, black olives
  and red pepper strips. Its food references add the whole fan-shaped
  fish on an oval steel tray ringed with tomato, cucumber, onion and
  lemon, with small bowls of pickles and sauces. Use the
  restaurant-plated look when a brief matches the archive; use the
  tray-over-bread form for the riverside venue.
- Primary protein/composition: large Tigris carp or barbs (shabout),
  skin-on.
- Side dishes/condiments: lemon, chopped onion and tomato, clay-oven
  flatbreads; no rice by default.
- Serving format: a whole or half flat fish on a tray over flatbread; the
  bread is the starch.
- Serving vessel: a large round steel tray, 40–50cm [EDITORIAL], lined with
  flatbread.
- Utensils: bread torn by hand, a fork to help.
- Visual/plating characteristics: butterflied flat, scored crosshatch, an
  amber-gold turmeric-tamarind glaze, a crisp blistered skin edge, moist
  white flake; embers and smoke behind. [MEDIUM-HIGH · S42]
- Real-world scale (§4.5): fish 40–60cm whole [LOW — flag]; a half portion
  ~20–30cm, about 2–2.5 can heights long. [LOW]
- Common confusion: not deep-fried fish; not a salmon steak; not a kebab
  platter.
- Confidence: HIGH for identity and method; LOW for exact size.
- Sources: [S6]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL shares]:

#### Dish: Quzi (qozi — festive lamb + rice)
- Prompt-ready line: "Slow-roast lamb joint with crisp brown crust atop a
  large oval mound of fluffy rice scattered with raisins and nuts, on a
  steel platter. No skewers, no sauce pool."
- Baghdad serving: a shared platter for the table with the 1.75 L bottle, never a single portion or the 250 mL glass (HARD STAGING RULES).
- Prompt-ready line (briefing look): "A large round polished steel tray,
  about twice the bottle's height across, heaped with yellow basmati
  under a thick layer of browned vermicelli rice studded with green
  peas, diced carrot, blanched almonds and raisins, topped by two
  glossy dark-brown slow-roast lamb shanks with the bone ends showing.
  No small plate, no skewers."
- Category: Celebration and Friday centrepiece.
- Cuisine lineage: Native Iraqi / Arab festive.
- Primary protein/composition: slow-roast whole lamb or shoulder (stuffed)
  over rice with nuts and raisins. [HIGH — Iraqi cuisine]
- Side dishes/condiments: roasted nuts, raisins, sometimes peas or carrot;
  jajik and turshi alongside.
- Authentic preparation (added 2026-10-09): lamb (today usually shanks or
  a shoulder; once a whole stuffed lamb) is boiled and skimmed, simmered
  with whole spices (cinnamon, cardamom, bay, sometimes dried lime) for
  1–2 hours, then roasted covered in some of its broth until it falls off
  the bone, and uncovered at the end to brown. Basmati is cooked in the
  lamb broth with turmeric or saffron. The topping is browned vermicelli
  (sha'riya), fried in oil with cardamom and cloves, mixed with blanched
  almonds and raisins, and often peas and diced carrot. To serve, the rice
  is mounded, the meat set on top, the vermicelli mix spread over it.
  Served with a white bean or okra stew on the side. [MEDIUM · S50]

- Common confusion: not a shawarma pile; not a layered biryani pot (quzi

- Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Sources: [S7]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL]: meat ~30%, rice ~55%,

#### Dish: Laham kass (لحم كص — "meat kass", Iraqi shawarma in samoon)
- Prompt-ready line: "One diamond-shaped Iraqi samoon loaf about the
  length of the glass bottle, split along its side and packed with
  thin, crisp-edged ribbons of browned beef shawarma, red tomato pieces,
  onion and parsley, with a thin yellow line of mango amba, on plain
  white paper, a lemon wedge beside it. No pita, no wrap, no falafel."
- Category: Everyday street food; lunch, late night, on the go.
- Cuisine lineage: Iraqi member of the vertical-spit family (döner,
  shawarma). Iraqis kept the name "kass" (from the verb "to cut", for the
  knife shaving the spit), spelled كص, قص or گص; "guss" in some
  transliterations. [MEDIUM · S51]
- Authentic preparation: thin slices of beef or veal (sometimes lamb),
  marinated in vinegar or lemon, onion and a kass spice mix (bharat), are
  stacked on a vertical spit, layered with slices of lamb fat, and roasted
  in front of an upright grill. The cook shaves the browned outside off
  with a long knife onto a griddle, sometimes giving it a final toss. Home
  versions bake thin slices layered with fat in a tray. [MEDIUM · S52]
- Serving format: **sandwich** default: one samoon split along its side
  and stuffed. A plate version (meat with bread on the side) exists; give
  it its own scene if a brief asks.
- Fillings: shaved meat, tomato, raw onion (often with parsley and
  sumac), pickles, amba (yellow mango pickle sauce), sometimes hummus or
  garlic sauce, a lemon wedge on the side. [MEDIUM · S53]
- Serving vessel: plain white paper, a small wooden board or a slate; a
  pickle saucer (cucumber pickles, tomato) beside it.
- Visual/plating characteristics: the diamond samoon (pointed both ends,
  golden, slightly blistered, sometimes sesame or nigella) opened along
  one side like a boat, packed with browned, shaggy, thin ribbons of meat
  with crisp edges, red tomato pieces, white onion, green parsley, and
  a ribbon of yellow amba. [HIGH for the samoon form; MEDIUM for fillings · S54]
- Real-world scale (§4.5): samoon ~20–25cm long, ~9–11cm wide at the
  middle [MEDIUM-LOW — flag], so about the height of the glass bottle
  laid flat; filling ~3–4cm proud of the bread.
- Common confusion: not a rolled shawarma wrap (thin lavash), not a pita
  pocket, not a Turkish döner in a round pide. The briefing's archive
  image had falafel in this bread; the brief replaces it with meat.
- Confidence: MEDIUM.
- Sources: [S9]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL shares]:
  - What dominates: **the samoon** ~55% of the view, meat ~30%, tomato,
    onion, parsley and amba ~15%.
  - Components: one samoon ~22cm; meat in thin ribbons 3–6cm long; tomato
    in 1–2cm pieces or half-slices; onion slivers; a 1cm-wide amba line;

| **Crispy ("Kentucky" style)** | Double-dipped in seasoned flour (often with a wet dip between), open-fried | Thick, shaggy, craggy crust with deep crevices and flakes that break off, loose crumbs on the board, very crunchy | Deep golden with darker tips |
| **Spicy** | Either of the above with chili and paprika in marinade and coating | As its base style, with red-orange specks through the crust | Golden orange to reddish brown |

[MEDIUM for the broast-vs-open-fried difference; EDITORIAL for marinade detail · S76]

**Cuts and what each looks like:**

| Cut | Size and shape | Visual tell |
|---|---|---|
| Drumstick | ~12cm, club shape, bone knuckle showing | The most recognisable piece, put it front |
| Thigh | ~9×8cm, rounded block | Thickest crust, darkest colour |
| Breast (half, on the bone) | ~12×8cm, wedge with a rib edge | Pale juicy meat when bitten or cut |
| Wing | ~10cm in a V, or split into drumette and flat | Thin pieces, crust very crisp at the tips |
| Strips / tenders | ~10×3cm, flat fingers | Boneless, even crumb, in rows |
| Bites | 2–4cm nuggets | Used in Rizo-style bowls and kids' meals |
| Fillet | whole thigh or breast, ~12cm | The zinger sandwich filling |

**How it's ordered (the scene unit):**
- **Solo:** a two- or three-piece meal (drumstick + thigh, or 3 strips)
  with fries, one samoon or a small bun, a cup of garlic sauce (toum,
  white and whipped), pickles (cucumber, pink turnip), sometimes
  coleslaw. [MEDIUM — menus]
- **Two people:** half a chicken (4 pieces: drumstick, thigh, wing,
  breast) with fries, samoon and sauces; or a mixed box of pieces and
  strips.
- **Family or friends:** a whole chicken (8 pieces) plus strips and wings
  tipped onto a tray lined with plain paper, a heap of fries, 2–3
  samoon, several sauce cups (garlic, chili, ketchup). A Baghdad chicken
  shop's posted meal: half chicken with sides, samoon, sauces and a
  drink. [MEDIUM · S77]
- Condiments are always on the side in small cups, never poured on.
  The meal is eaten by hand: pieces pulled apart, dipped in garlic sauce,
  stuffed into torn samoon with pickles.

- Serving vessel: a steel wire basket (as in the briefing), a paper-lined
  steel tray, a plain cardboard box with the lid open at home, or a
  plastic oval plate in a broast shop.
- Visual/plating characteristics: depends on the style above. Always
  show at least one drumstick for recognition, a mix of cut shapes (not
  identical pieces), steam, and a white sauce cup for the Iraqi garlic
  sauce. [HIGH for what the briefing shows; MEDIUM for the rest]
- Real-world scale (§4.5): drumstick ~12cm (about half the bottle
  height); basket ~20×12cm; samoon ~22cm.
- Common confusion: not Korean glazed chicken (no gloss, no sesame), not
  nuggets-only, not Southern US with biscuits and gravy, not a chain
  bucket.
- Confidence: MEDIUM.
- Sources: [S17]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL]:
  - What dominates: **chicken** ~60%, fries ~20%, bread ~10%, sauces and
    pickles ~10% (solo or pair meal). For the basket-only archive look:
    chicken ~80%, sauce pot ~10%, crumbs ~10%.
  - Components: per the ordering unit above; sauce cups ~6cm; 4–6 pickle
    slices.
  - Arrangement: pieces heaped with the drumstick on top facing camera;
    fries beside, not under; sauce cups at the front edge; samoon at the
    back.
  - State cues: steam, oil glint on the crust, a bitten or torn piece
    showing juicy white meat (optional), crumbs on the paper.
  - Absent on purpose: chain bucket or box print, glaze, sesame, gravy,
    biscuits, pork.

#### Dish: Pizza, Iraqi pizzeria style (بيتزا)
- Prompt-ready line: "A round 30cm pizza, about one and a half bottle
  heights across, cut into eight slices on a round wooden board, with a
  golden puffed rim and melted cheese scattered with spiced chicken
  pieces, black olive rings and green and red pepper strips. No pork."
- Category: Casual dining and delivery; evening, friends and families.
- Cuisine lineage: Italian import sold mostly by mixed fast-food and café
  menus; chicken toppings and Turkish-style pizza are common.
  Baghdad's best-known early pizzeria opened in 2003. [MEDIUM · S78]
- Authentic preparation: medium-thick hand-stretched crust baked in a
  deck or stone oven; tomato sauce, mozzarella; the briefing's two
  versions are a chicken and vegetable pizza (spiced chicken pieces, black
  olives, green and red peppers, tomato, onion) and a pepperoni pizza.
  **Pepperoni in Iraq is beef or chicken, never pork.** [MEDIUM for
  toppings; HIGH for the halal rule]
- Serving vessel: a round wooden board or a round aluminium tray, cut in
  6–8 slices.
- Visual/plating characteristics: a golden puffed rim, melted cheese,
  toppings scattered right to the rim; the pepperoni version is a dense
  red slice layer with slightly cupped edges. [HIGH — briefing frames]
- Real-world scale (§4.5): ~30cm medium, about one and a half bottle
  heights across.
- Common confusion: not Neapolitan leopard-spotted; not a deep-dish.
- Confidence: MEDIUM.
- Sources: [S18]
- Composition & proportions (§4.7) [EDITORIAL]:
  - What dominates: **cheese and toppings** ~80%, crust rim ~20%.
  - Components: chicken pieces 1–2cm; olive rings; pepper strips; or
    ~25–30 pepperoni slices 4cm.
  - Arrangement: even scatter, slice cuts visible.
  - State cues: melted cheese pull, oil sheen.
  - Absent on purpose: pork, ham, bacon, basil-leaf Neapolitan styling.

#### Dish: Creamy chicken fettuccine pasta (پاستا / باستا)
- Prompt-ready line: "A tall twisted nest of fettuccine in a glossy
  cream and parmesan sauce in a wide white bowl with a dark rim, flecked
  with chopped parsley and grated cheese, a fork resting in it, the
  nest about half the bottle's height. No bacon."
- Category: Café and casual dining; evening.
- Cuisine lineage: Italian-American café dish. No Iraq-specific source was
  found; it's here because the briefing lists it. Iraqi home pasta is
  usually a baked macaroni with meat and béchamel or a tomato meat sauce;
  café pasta is the cream style. [LOW — flag for SME]
- Authentic preparation: fettuccine tossed in a cream, butter and parmesan
  sauce, often with sautéed chicken and mushrooms; finished with chopped
  parsley and grated cheese. [LOW for Iraq-specific frequency]
- Serving vessel: a wide white rimmed bowl with a dark rim line (as in the
  briefing), a fork resting in it.
- Visual/plating characteristics: a high twisted nest of pale ribbon pasta
  with a creamy coat, green parsley flecks, a dusting of grated cheese.
  [HIGH — briefing frame]
- Real-world scale (§4.5): bowl ~26–28cm; nest ~8cm high.
- Common confusion: not spaghetti bolognese; no wine or candles.
- Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM (look HIGH, local frequency LOW).
- Sources: [S19]

  - What dominates: **pasta** ~85%, herbs and cheese ~10%, chicken ~5%
